SCENARIO RANKINGS (by Anticipated Bundling performance vs competition):

1. IMPOSSIBLE DEADLINE - DECISIVE WIN ★★★★★
   Anticipated: 34.5 min avg delivery time
   All Others:  39.5 min avg delivery time
   Advantage: 5 MINUTES FASTER (12.7% improvement)
   
   Analysis: Proactive dispatch critical. Only Anticipated can dispatch the
   courier BEFORE the order is ready, perfectly timing arrival with food
   completion. This is the "impossible deadline" that reactive algorithms
   structurally cannot solve within the time constraint.

2. RIVER DIVIDE - STRONG PERFORMANCE ★★★★☆
   Network Bundling: 140 orders (WINNER throughput)
   Simple Bundling:  135 orders  
   Anticipated:      134 orders (3rd place, -4% vs winner)

   Anticipated:      259 km (BEST, -17% vs Network)
   Network:          314 km
   
   Analysis: Anticipated achieves nearly same throughput with drastically
   lower distance. Geographic bottleneck (river with 2 bridges) makes
   route efficiency critical. Anticipated's holistic cost function optimizes
   crossing patterns better than pure bundling heuristics.

3. POPUP PROBLEM - MODERATE PERFORMANCE ★★★☆☆
   Simple Bundling:  158 orders (WINNER throughput)
   Network Bundling: 156 orders
   Anticipated:      151 orders (3rd place, -4% vs winner)
   
   Distance efficiency:
   Anticipated:      277 km (BEST, -9% vs Simple)
   Simple:           303 km
   
   Analysis: Unpredictable bursts favor reactive bundling. Anticipated's
   lookahead window (300s) may miss sudden spikes. Distance efficiency

4. DOWNTOWN CRUSH - UNDERPERFORMANCE ★★☆☆☆
   Simple Bundling:  139 orders (WINNER throughput)

   Anticipated:      128 orders (3rd place, -8% vs winner)

   Simple:           224 km
   
   Analysis: Concentrated downtown demand creates abundant same-restaurant
   bundling opportunities. Anticipated's CP-SAT solver overhead may hurt
   throughput under high load. Simple Bundling's greedy same-restaurant
   heuristic wins in dense urban scenarios.

================================================================================
ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS: WHY IS ANTICIPATED UNDERPERFORMING?
================================================================================

HYPOTHESIS 1: THROUGHPUT PRIORITIZATION TOO AGGRESSIVE
- Score = (n × 1M) - EffectiveCost
- With 1M multiplier, algorithm ALWAYS prefers bundles over singles
- May be forming inefficient 3-order bundles that delay service
- Evidence: Fewer bundles (45-61) vs Simple (60-79) but LOWER throughput

HYPOTHESIS 2: PENALTY VALUES TOO WEAK (CONFIRMED BY USER EDIT)
- User changed penalties:
  - ALPHA_PENALTY: 0.5 → 0.1  (5x weaker)
  - BETA_PENALTY:  0.3 → 0.15 (2x weaker)
  - STALENESS_BONUS: 0.4 → 0.45 (slightly stronger)
  
- Weak penalties mean temporal intelligence has minimal impact
- Algorithm defaults to aggressive bundling without penalty deterrence

HYPOTHESIS 3: CP-SAT SOLVER OVERHEAD
- Network Bundling also uses CP-SAT but outperforms Anticipated
- Difference: Anticipated evaluates MORE candidates (READY + PENDING orders)
- At 17+ orders threshold, switches to geographic clustering
- May be hitting threshold more often due to lookahead window

HYPOTHESIS 4: DELIVERY TIME PARADOX
- Anticipated has BEST distance efficiency (190-277 km)
- But WORST delivery times (27-31 min, worse than Hungarian's 22-25 min)
- Possible causes:
  a) Bundling delays: Waiting for future orders increases individual delay
  b) TSP route complexity: 3-order bundles have longer service chains
  c) Proactive dispatch backfiring: Courier wait time not penalized enough

================================================================================
WHAT IS GOING ON? (Executive Summary)
================================================================================

Anticipated Bundling is OVER-OPTIMIZED for distance efficiency at the
expense of throughput and delivery time.

The algorithm successfully minimizes distance traveled (-9% to -17% vs
competition) but achieves this by:
1. Aggressive bundling (throughput-first framework)
2. Complex multi-restaurant routes (TSP optimization)
3. Proactive dispatch (lookahead window)

However, these strategies BACKFIRE in 3 out of 4 scenarios:
- Bundling delays individual orders (longer delivery times)
- CP-SAT overhead reduces responsiveness (lower throughput)
- Weak penalties fail to prevent bad temporal tradeoffs

Only in the "Impossible Deadline" scenario—specifically designed to require
proactive dispatch—does Anticipated's anticipatory intelligence provide
decisive advantage.

RECOMMENDATION:
The current penalty values (α=0.1, β=0.15) are too weak. The algorithm needs
stronger temporal penalties to balance throughput maximization with delivery
time quality.

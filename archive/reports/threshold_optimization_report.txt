THEORETICAL ANALYSIS vs EMPIRICAL RESULTS
================================================================================

SIMPLE BUNDLING (Partition Problem):
  Theoretical Recommendation: threshold = 9 orders
    - 9 orders = 21,147 partitions (fast ~100-200ms)
    - 10 orders = 115,975 partitions (5.5x explosion)
  
  Implementation: threshold = 9 ✓ ACCEPTED
  Impact: No measurable change (scenarios don't hit 9+ orders at single restaurant)
  Conclusion: Safe increase from 6→9, provides headroom for edge cases

NETWORK BUNDLING (Combination Problem):
  Theoretical Recommendation: threshold = 18 orders
    - 18 orders = 987 bundle candidates (sweet spot)
    - 19 orders = 1,159 candidates (17% increase)
  
  Empirical Testing Results (threshold 17→18):
    popup_problem:    132→130 orders (-1.5%)
    downtown_crush:   103→100 orders (-2.9%)
    river_divide:     102→96 orders  (-5.9%)
  
  Implementation: threshold = 17 ✗ REVERTED
  Conclusion: Theory predicted 18 as computational sweet spot, but empirical
              data shows 17 is OPTIMAL for solution quality. Hypothesis: larger
              candidate pool introduces suboptimal bundles that Hungarian
              algorithm selects, degrading global solution quality.

ANTICIPATED BUNDLING (Combination Problem + Lookahead):
  Theoretical Recommendation: threshold = 18 orders (matching Network)
  
  Empirical Testing Results (threshold 17→18):
    popup_problem:    134→126 orders (-6.0%)
    downtown_crush:   103→98 orders  (-4.9%)
    river_divide:     97→95 orders   (-2.1%)
  
  Implementation: threshold = 17 ✗ REVERTED
  Conclusion: Same as Network - quality degradation with larger candidate pool

KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. THEORY vs PRACTICE DIVERGENCE:
   Theoretical "computational sweet spot" (threshold=18) does not equal
   empirical "solution quality sweet spot" (threshold=17). Hungarian algorithm
   performs better with constrained, high-quality candidate pools than with
   larger, noisier pools.

2. SIMPLE BUNDLING THRESHOLD SAFE INCREASE:
   6→9 threshold increase provides safety margin with no performance impact.

3. HIERARCHY VALIDATION:
   Algorithm superiority holds in 2/3 scenarios. River divide is a legitimate
   edge case where geographic constraints favor simpler bundling.

4. DISTANCE EFFICIENCY vs THROUGHPUT TRADEOFF:
   Anticipated consistently achieves best distance efficiency, sometimes at
   cost of raw throughput. This reflects its optimization for holistic cost
   (route + wait + delay) rather than pure throughput maximization.

5. NO COMBINATORIAL EXPLOSIONS:
   All scenarios complete in ~50-60s with stable performance. Thresholds
   successfully prevent explosion while maintaining near-optimal solutions.
